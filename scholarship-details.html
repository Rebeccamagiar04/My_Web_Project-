About the Displaced Undergraduate Fund

The University of Edinburgh has developed Education Beyond Borders, an initiative intended to improve access to higher education for displaced students and academics. Through partnerships with organisations working with displaced communities, the University aims to reduce barriers that prevent talented people from continuing their education.

In July 2025, the University announced a partnership with Refugee Education UK to co-fund and support two female undergraduates from South Sudan who were living in Kakuma Refugee Camp in Kenya. The announced pathway was designed to help these students enter an International Foundation programme and then continue to undergraduate degree programmes.

This announcement is particularly relevant to students in refugee-hosting communities because it demonstrates a university partnership specifically designed around the educational barriers faced by displaced young people.

Important: The announcement describes a specific partnership and a limited number of students. It should not be interpreted as confirmation that the University is currently accepting applications from all refugees or international applicants.

What Is Education Beyond Borders?

Education Beyond Borders is the University of Edinburgh's wider initiative to expand educational access and support for displaced students and academics. Its activities include scholarships, partnerships, academic support, and other projects intended to help displaced scholars participate in higher education.

The initiative is broader than this undergraduate pathway. Other activities under Education Beyond Borders may have different academic levels, eligibility rules, funding arrangements, and application processes.

Applicants should therefore make sure they are reading information about the displaced undergraduate partnership rather than assuming that the requirements for a postgraduate or online scholarship also apply to undergraduate applicants.

The Role of Refugee Education UK

Refugee Education UK (REUK) is an organisation that works to improve access to education for young refugees and displaced people. It provides educational support and works with partners to address the practical and structural barriers that can prevent displaced young people from reaching further and higher education.

In the announced Edinburgh partnership, REUK works alongside the University to support the displaced undergraduate pathway. Prospective applicants should contact the relevant organisations to find out whether REUK is currently identifying candidates, accepting enquiries, or preparing a new intake.

Do not assume that submitting a general enquiry to REUK automatically counts as a formal scholarship application.

Understanding the Foundation-to-Degree Pathway

The published announcement describes a route beginning with an International Foundation programme, followed by progression to undergraduate degree studies.

A foundation programme is designed to prepare students for university-level education. Depending on the programme, this preparation may include academic skills, subject knowledge, English-language development, and familiarity with the expectations of university study.

The foundation stage should not automatically be treated as the first year of a bachelor's degree. Applicants must ask the providers how long the pathway lasts, what the foundation qualification leads to, and which degree programmes are available after successful completion.

Before accepting a place, confirm the progression conditions, required academic results, English-language requirements, and whether the scholarship continues throughout the subsequent degree.

About DAAD Scholarships

The German Academic Exchange Service (DAAD) is one of the major organisations supporting international academic exchange. It offers scholarships for university students, graduates, doctoral candidates, researchers, and academics.

Opportunities include postgraduate study in Germany, development-related master's programmes, research grants, summer courses, and master's or doctoral study at selected universities in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Important: DAAD does not offer one universal scholarship with the same requirements for everyone. Most full-degree opportunities are for postgraduate study, so school leavers seeking a bachelor's degree should check each programme carefully.

Featured Scholarship Opportunities

1. Study Scholarships for STEM Disciplines

Supports eligible graduates from developing and emerging countries pursuing a full-time master's degree in mathematics, computer science, natural sciences, or engineering at a German state or state-recognised university.

  • Study level: Master's degree.
  • Duration: Usually 10–24 months, depending on the programme.
  • Monthly stipend: €992.
  • Additional benefits: Health and liability insurance, an annual study allowance of €460, and a possible travel allowance. Other support may be available under specific conditions.
  • Tuition: The selected master's programme must be tuition-free; DAAD does not pay tuition fees under this scholarship.
  • Eligibility: An appropriate bachelor's degree, strong academic results, and the required language proficiency. The last university degree generally must not be older than six years.
  • 2026 deadline: 30 September 2026 — passed.

Supports eligible graduates pursuing selected master's or doctoral programmes at participating universities in their home country or elsewhere in Sub-Saharan Africa. The programme particularly aims to strengthen higher education and develop skilled professionals in the region.

  • Study level: Master's and PhD, depending on the call.
  • Duration: Generally up to two years for master's programmes; doctoral funding depends on the programme.
  • Possible benefits: Monthly allowance, study and research allowance, printing allowance, and tuition fees paid to the institution.

  • Eligibility: A relevant first university degree and compliance with the requirements of a participating programme.
  • 2026 call: The published application deadline was 29 April 2026 — the call is closed.

The EPOS programme supports qualified graduates from eligible developing countries to pursue selected development-related postgraduate courses at participating universities in Germany. Courses are selected because of their relevance to development and professional capacity-building.

  • Study level: Selected postgraduate courses, primarily master's degrees.
  • Eligibility: Academic qualifications, professional experience, country eligibility, and other requirements specified by the individual course.

In Kenya, DAFI supports eligible refugee students pursuing higher education at participating institutions. The programme is particularly important for students who have completed secondary school but face financial barriers to university or other eligible tertiary studies.

DAFI is competitive, and the availability of scholarships depends on funding, the annual selection process, and the requirements of the country programme.

About the Programme

The Student Refugee Program (SRP) is managed by World University Service of Canada (WUSC), a Canadian international development organisation. Established in 1978, the programme combines access to post-secondary education with refugee resettlement to Canada.

It provides eligible young refugees with an opportunity to continue their education at participating Canadian universities, colleges, and CEGEPs. CEGEPs are post-secondary institutions found primarily in Quebec.

Unlike an ordinary scholarship, SRP also includes a resettlement pathway. Selected students are supported through Canada's Private Sponsorship of Refugees framework, subject to assessment and final approval by Canadian immigration authorities.

WUSC works with local partner organisations in countries where applications are accepted. In Canada, campus-based WUSC Local Committees help provide financial, academic, and social support to arriving students.

Important: SRP facilitates undergraduate study only. It is not a direct-entry master's or PhD scholarship, and selection by WUSC does not automatically guarantee approval to travel to Canada.

Countries Where Applications Are Accepted

WUSC currently lists the following countries of asylum for its Student Refugee Program:

  • Kenya
  • Uganda
  • Jordan
  • Lebanon

Applicants must be living in one of these participating countries and satisfy its residence, registration, academic, and other programme requirements. The programme is not open to applicants living anywhere in the world.

In Kenya, applications may be accepted from eligible refugees living in urban areas or in Kakuma and Dadaab refugee camps. The current call determines the precise requirements and application process.

Eligibility Criteria

For the 2027–2028 Kenya intake, applicants were required to meet the following general conditions:

  • Be registered with UNHCR and possess the required refugee registration documentation.
  • Have the Government of Kenya refugee identification card and original proof of registration required by the call.
  • Be living as a refugee in Kenya and have continuously resided in Kenya for at least three years at the time of application.
  • Have arrived in Kenya before 1 January 2023 for the 2027–2028 intake.
  • Be between 18 and 25 years old as of 1 January 2026.
  • Have completed secondary school and possess the required original academic certificates and result documents.
  • Meet the minimum academic grade for the country and secondary school qualification concerned.
  • Demonstrate sufficient English proficiency for undergraduate studies in Canada.
  • Be single, unmarried, and without children.
  • Face barriers to accessing or completing undergraduate post-secondary education.
  • Be considered eligible and suitable for resettlement under Canada's Private Sponsorship of Refugees framework.
  • Demonstrate maturity, self-reliance, and suitability for living and studying independently in Canada.

These requirements are specific to the published 2027–2028 Kenya call. Requirements for future intakes may change.

The 2027–2028 Kenya call published minimum academic requirements according to the country where the applicant completed secondary education. The following are examples from that call:

This is a selection of the published requirements, not the full country-by-country table. Applicants with qualifications from other countries may also be considered if they satisfy the other requirements. Consult the official Kenya call for the complete table and any applicable exceptions.

Academic eligibility is only one part of the assessment. Meeting the minimum grade does not guarantee shortlisting or selection.

What the Programme Provides

SRP is not a conventional cash scholarship paid directly to a student. Support is organised through WUSC, participating educational institutions, and campus Local Committees.

During the initial 12-month sponsorship period, support can include:

  • Free or reduced tuition and accommodation costs, arranged through the programme and participating institution.
  • Financial assistance for basic necessities.
  • Academic support and guidance as students adjust to their studies.
  • Social support from a campus-based WUSC Local Committee.
  • Assistance with integration into the Canadian university or college community.
  • Support with the programme's resettlement and pre-departure process.

The financial arrangements may include in-kind assistance rather than unrestricted cash payments. Applicants should not assume that every personal expense will be paid directly or that the same package applies at every institution.

After the initial 12 months, students are expected to work towards greater financial independence. Possible sources of continued support include institutional bursaries, scholarships, student loans where appropriate, and permitted employment income. Students should discuss their options with their institution and Local Committee.

Academic Opportunities in Canada

SRP facilitates admission to undergraduate studies at participating Canadian post-secondary institutions. Available courses and placements depend on institutional participation, admission requirements, and the programme's placement process.

Students may be placed in different provinces and communities. Applicants should be prepared to study and live wherever an appropriate placement is available; placement in a particular city, institution, or near family members is not guaranteed.

Students must meet the academic admission requirements of their assigned institution and be prepared to continue their studies successfully after the initial sponsorship period.

Application Process

  1. Read the latest WUSC SRP call for your country of asylum.
  2. Check every eligibility condition, including residence duration, age, marital and parental status, academic grades, and documentation.
  3. Prepare your original academic records and refugee identification documents.
  4. Complete the official online application form during the published application window.
  5. Upload all required documents as clear electronic files in the accepted format.
  6. Review every answer and attachment before submitting the form.
  7. Keep a copy of the submitted application and confirmation for your records.
  8. Monitor your email and follow instructions from WUSC or its country partner.
  9. If shortlisted, participate in the further assessment stages and provide any additional information requested.

Applications are accepted only during the official recruitment period. WUSC does not accept referrals from individuals as a substitute for applying through the official process.

Selection and Shortlisting

After the application deadline, WUSC reviews applications to determine whether candidates satisfy the programme's eligibility requirements. Only shortlisted applicants are contacted to progress to subsequent selection stages.

The process is competitive and considers eligibility, academic readiness, the barriers applicants face in accessing higher education, and suitability for the programme and resettlement. The precise stages and assessments are determined by WUSC's selection process for the relevant intake.

Submitting an application does not guarantee an interview, shortlisting, selection, admission to a particular institution, or approval for resettlement.

WUSC states that it does not set nationality-based quotas for applicants. Refugees of different nationalities may apply as long as they meet the requirements of a participating country.

Resettlement to Canada

A distinctive feature of SRP is its connection to Canada's Private Sponsorship of Refugees framework. WUSC selects eligible candidates and supports the resettlement process with its partners in Canada.

The process can take two years or longer. Final immigration decisions are made by Canadian immigration authorities, not by WUSC alone. Candidates must satisfy the relevant immigration requirements and admissibility checks before travel is approved.

Students who are approved through the programme arrive in Canada as permanent residents. Permanent residence gives them a different immigration status from that of an ordinary international student. They may later be eligible to apply for Canadian citizenship if they satisfy the applicable legal requirements.

Selection by WUSC is not a guarantee of a visa, travel approval, or admission to Canada. If Canadian authorities refuse a candidate's application or deny entry, the candidate's participation may be cancelled.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is WUSC SRP a scholarship?

WUSC describes SRP as a refugee resettlement programme that includes access to post-secondary education. It provides financial and social support during the initial sponsorship period, but it is not a conventional scholarship that simply pays a student cash.

Can someone apply directly from their country of origin?

Applicants must be living in one of WUSC's participating countries of asylum and meet the requirements of that country's application call. The programme is not a general application route for all students living anywhere in the world.

Can married applicants or applicants with children apply?

The published 2027–2028 Kenya call required applicants to be single, unmarried, and without children. Check future calls for any changes to this requirement.

Can a student choose the university or city in Canada?

Does WUSC guarantee permanent residence?

No. WUSC facilitates the sponsorship process, but Canadian immigration authorities make the final immigration and admissibility decisions.

Can students receive support after the first year?

Students are expected to work towards financial independence after the first 12 months. They can explore institutional scholarships, bursaries, other assistance, and permitted employment options with their institution.

Can applicants with disabilities apply?

WUSC encourages eligible refugee youth with disabilities to apply. Applicants who need accommodations to complete the application should contact the relevant WUSC office or country partner.

About UNICORE

UNICORE stands for University Corridors for Refugees. It is an initiative coordinated by the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), in partnership with Italian universities, the Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, and other organisations.

The initiative aims to expand access to higher education for refugees living in countries of first asylum. It creates a safe and regular pathway for selected students to travel to Italy and continue their university education.

Participating universities offer master's scholarships and manage their own academic selection processes. UNHCR and programme partners support the wider initiative and refugee verification arrangements.

Unlike an ordinary international student admission route, UNICORE is specifically designed for eligible refugees who meet the requirements of the relevant call and participating university.

The programme does not automatically grant a place to every refugee applicant. Selection is competitive, and applicants are assessed according to academic merit, motivation, programme suitability, and the requirements published by the university.

Official UNICORE Programme Information

Level of Study and Duration

UNICORE primarily supports eligible refugees pursuing a second-cycle university qualification, which corresponds to a master's degree in the Italian higher education system.

  • Applicants must hold officially recognized refugee status (in collaboration with UNHCR) and reside in one of the designated host countries (e.g., Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia, South Africa, Nigeria, Mozambique, Tanzania, Zambia, Zimbabwe).
  • Candidates must hold a Bachelor's degree awarded by a recognized higher education institution (typically obtained within the last 5 years).
  • Applicants must demonstrate strong academic achievement with a Minimum Cumulative Grade Point Average (GPA) of at least 3.0 on a 4.0 scale (or equivalent to at least 24/30 under the Italian grading system).
  • Candidates must meet the specific entry and academic course requirements for their chosen Master's degree program at participating Italian host universities.
  • Applicants must have never before been enrolled in a degree program at any of the partner universities participating in the UNICORE project.
  • Candidates must demonstrate English language proficiency at CEFR Level B2 or higher through official test certificates or proof of an English-medium undergraduate degree.
  • Applicants may apply to a maximum of two participating Italian universities per call for applications cycle.
  • Candidates must successfully pass both the initial documentation evaluation and an online interview conducted by the university's expert selection committee.

Applicants must hold a qualification that is valid for admission to their chosen master's programme. A bachelor's degree alone does not guarantee eligibility: the degree, grades, subject background, and any additional admission requirements must satisfy the university's rules.

UNICORE is not a general undergraduate scholarship for students who have only completed secondary school.

About the Chinese Government Scholarship

The Chinese Government Scholarship, commonly called the CSC Scholarship, supports eligible international students who want to study at participating universities in China. It is administered by the China Scholarship Council on behalf of the Chinese government.

Scholarships may be available for undergraduate degrees, master's degrees, doctoral degrees, Chinese-language study and certain visiting or non-degree programmes.

The programme is not one single application with identical rules for everyone. Applications may be handled through Chinese diplomatic missions, designated government authorities or participating universities, depending on the scholarship category.

As a result, applicants must confirm the eligibility conditions, agency details, funding package and deadline for the particular route they intend to use.

What Does the Scholarship Cover?

A full Chinese Government Scholarship commonly includes several of the following benefits, subject to the applicable scholarship category and award terms:

  • Tuition fees: A tuition waiver for the approved study programme.
  • Accommodation: University accommodation or an accommodation subsidy, depending on the applicable scholarship arrangements.
  • Monthly stipend: A living allowance according to the study level and official scholarship terms.
  • Medical insurance: Medical insurance or comprehensive medical coverage specified by the scholarship.
  • Preparatory language study: Some students may receive Chinese-language preparation before beginning their academic programme, if required and included in their award.

Important about travel: Do not assume that international flights are covered. Some official CSC calls explicitly require students to pay their own international airfare. Check the specific award terms before accepting an offer.

The exact stipend, accommodation arrangement and other benefits must be confirmed in the current official scholarship announcement. Not every programme administered by CSC has the same funding package.

Application Routes: Which One Should You Use?

Before preparing an application, identify the correct application route. This is particularly important for international applicants and refugees living outside their country of origin.

Route A: Embassy or Government-Nominated Scholarship

Some CSC scholarships are administered through a Chinese embassy or a designated government authority in a country. The authority may set its own eligibility conditions, documents, agency number, nomination process and deadline.

An embassy-nominated opportunity may be restricted to citizens of the country concerned. If an announcement says it is for Kenyan citizens or Kenyan students applying through Kenya's Ministry of Education, a South Sudanese refugee living in Kenya must not assume that they qualify.

Route B: University-Based Application

Some participating universities accept applications for CSC scholarships through their own designated processes. Applicants must identify universities that offer their intended course and confirm whether international applicants of their nationality and residence status can use that route.

A university-based route may still require a CSC online application, a pre-admission document, academic documents and separate university admission steps.

Route C: Special Government Scholarship Programmes

China also offers specialised scholarship schemes for particular groups, professional fields or postgraduate applicants. Some are intended for public servants, experienced professionals or candidates with specific qualifications.

These special programmes are not interchangeable with the general undergraduate scholarship. Check the exact eligibility requirements before choosing one.

General Eligibility Requirements

General CSC calls commonly require applicants to:

  • Be a citizen of a country other than the People's Republic of China.
  • Meet the academic qualifications required for the intended study level.
  • Meet the applicable age limit.
  • Be in good physical and mental health as required by the relevant call.
  • Meet the chosen university's academic and language requirements.
  • Submit the required documents in the requested format.
  • Follow the rules of the particular application authority or university.

These are general requirements, not a guarantee that every applicant qualifies for every CSC category. Individual calls can add further conditions.

Eligibility for Undergraduate Applicants

Applicants seeking a bachelor's degree generally need to have completed high school or an equivalent secondary qualification and meet the age and academic conditions specified by the application route and university.

Age Requirement

General CSC scholarship calls commonly set an undergraduate age limit of under 25 years at the time specified in the relevant announcement. The exact wording and age-calculation date must be checked in the current call.

Academic Qualification

Applicants generally need a recognised high-school qualification and sufficiently strong academic results. The CSC's general eligibility rules do not establish one universal secondary-school percentage that applies to every university and every country.

Universities may set their own minimum grades, required subjects or additional admission tests. Applicants must check the requirements for their intended degree.

China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA)

Beginning with the 2026/2027 academic year, the published CSC application guidance requires applicants for bachelor's programmes to take the China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA) and provide a valid score report.

Applicants should confirm the required CSCA subjects with their target university and major, and check the official CSCA website for current test dates and registration details. Do not rely on test dates from a previous admission cycle.

Language Requirements

Language requirements depend on the programme's teaching language. Chinese-taught courses may require Chinese proficiency, while English-taught courses may require accepted English-language evidence according to the university's rules.

Other Undergraduate Requirements

  • Completion of secondary education or an accepted equivalent qualification.
  • Academic transcripts and certificates.
  • A valid CSCA score report where required.
  • A study plan or personal statement if requested.
  • Any pre-admission document required by the chosen university or scholarship authority.

Note: A university may impose additional requirements for engineering, medicine, science or other specialised degrees.

Eligibility for Master's Applicants

Applicants for master's programmes generally need to hold a recognised bachelor's degree or equivalent qualification. General CSC calls commonly specify that master's applicants should be under 35 years old, although the precise wording in the applicable call must be followed.

Applicants should also:

  • Meet the university's academic entry requirements.
  • Provide bachelor's degree certificates and transcripts.
  • Meet the language requirements of the selected programme.
  • Submit a study plan or research proposal where required.
  • Provide recommendation letters where required by the university or scholarship category.

Some specialised scholarships may require professional experience, employment in a particular sector or nomination by a government authority. These requirements do not automatically apply to every general CSC master's route.

Eligibility for PhD Applicants

Doctoral applicants generally need a recognised master's degree or an equivalent qualification in a relevant field. General CSC calls commonly set a doctoral age limit of under 40 years, subject to the applicable call.

PhD applicants may need to provide:

  • Master's degree certificate and academic transcripts.
  • A detailed research proposal.
  • Recommendation letters from academic referees.
  • Evidence of language proficiency.
  • A pre-admission document or acceptance from a suitable academic department or supervisor, where required.

Research areas, supervisor availability and additional admission conditions vary between universities. Applicants should verify that their proposed research can be supported by the institution they select.

Language Requirements

Applicants must meet the language requirements of their chosen course. These requirements are not identical across all universities or scholarship categories.

Published general CSC calls commonly specify the following minimum Chinese-language levels for certain Chinese-taught programmes:

  • Chinese-taught undergraduate programmes: HSK Level 3 is commonly specified in general guidance, subject to the applicable programme rules.
  • Chinese-taught master's and doctoral programmes: HSK Level 4 is commonly specified in general guidance.

Some programmes may require a higher level. Applicants should not assume that these general levels are sufficient for every course.

For programmes taught in English, the university may request IELTS, TOEFL or another accepted proof of English proficiency, or may specify its own exemption rules.

A language test is not necessarily required in the same form by every university. Check the current admission instructions for the course and language of instruction you choose.
