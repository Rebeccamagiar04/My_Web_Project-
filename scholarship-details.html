Before You Apply: Understand What Common App Is

Common App is not a scholarship provider. It is an online platform that allows students to prepare one main undergraduate application and submit it to participating colleges and universities.

Each university independently decides whether to admit you, whether you qualify for financial aid and how much assistance you will receive.

The universities featured on this page have been selected because they have institutional need-based financial aid policies, substantial scholarship opportunities or participation in a recognised refugee and displaced-student initiative.

This is particularly important for students who cannot afford international tuition, accommodation, food and other university expenses without substantial financial assistance.

Understanding the Different Funding Policies

1. Need-blind admission and full demonstrated need

Need-blind admission means the university does not consider your family's ability to pay when deciding whether to admit you. Once admitted, the university commits to meeting 100% of your demonstrated financial need according to its own calculation.

This is one of the most attractive policies for international applicants who need substantial financial assistance.

2. Need-aware admission and full demonstrated need

Need-aware admission means the university considers your financial circumstances during the admission process. Requesting a large amount of financial aid may make admission more competitive.

However, some need-aware universities promise to meet 100% of the demonstrated financial need of students they admit and fund.

3. Need-based scholarships

These are grants or scholarships determined by your family's financial circumstances rather than academic achievement alone. They may cover a substantial portion of tuition and living expenses.

Some universities combine need-based assistance with merit scholarships and campus employment.

4. Merit-based scholarships

Merit scholarships recognise academic performance, leadership, extracurricular involvement, talent or other achievements. Some are substantial, but many cover only part of the total cost.

Do not assume that receiving a merit scholarship automatically means you can afford the remaining expenses.

Global Student Haven Initiative: A Dedicated Pathway for Displaced Students

The Global Student Haven Initiative is a collaboration between participating American universities intended to help refugees, displaced students and people affected by conflict or natural disasters access undergraduate education in the United States.

Its founding institutions are:

  • Bowdoin College
  • California Institute of Technology (Caltech)
  • Dartmouth College
  • New York University
  • Pomona College
  • Smith College
  • Trinity College, Connecticut
  • Williams College

Participating institutions commit to providing financial aid to first-year undergraduate students with demonstrated need whom they admit. The initiative also seeks to connect displaced students with support services after arrival, including appropriate housing and other campus assistance.

Important: Global Student Haven is not a separate scholarship that students receive simply by registering. You must still apply to the participating university and satisfy its admission and financial aid requirements.

The initiative is not exclusively restricted to refugees. Its participating institutions also aim to support other students affected by conflict, displacement and humanitarian crises.

The initiative primarily focuses on first-year undergraduate applicants. Financial aid for transfer applicants is determined separately by each institution. Graduate applicants are not covered by the initiative.

Understanding Acceptance Rates and Your Admission Chances

One of the most important things to consider before applying is how selective a university is.

A university may have excellent financial aid but receive thousands of applications for a limited number of places. This means that having a full-need financial aid policy does not automatically make admission easy.

How the acceptance-rate categories work

  • Below 5%: Extremely selective. Treat these universities as high-reach applications.
  • 5%–15%: Very competitive. Admission remains difficult, especially for applicants requiring substantial international financial aid.
  • Above 15%: Comparatively broader admission rates, but this does not mean admission is likely. Check the international admission rate separately.
  • Not reported: Do not assume the university has a high acceptance rate simply because no figure is available.

Important: A high acceptance rate is not a guarantee of funding

Some universities admit a larger proportion of international students but provide limited financial assistance. Others offer excellent funding but admit very few international applicants.

For students who cannot finance their education independently, the financial aid policy must remain a priority alongside the admission rate.

Do not choose a university simply because its acceptance rate looks attractive. First establish whether its financial aid can cover the expenses you cannot afford.

Why international acceptance rates matter

International students may be evaluated in a different admissions category from domestic students. At some institutions, international applicants requesting substantial aid compete within a particularly limited financial aid budget.

For example, Oberlin reports that approximately 8–10% of international applicants are admitted in a typical year. It also explains that applicants able to contribute at least $35,000 annually are more competitive in its international admission process.

Macalester similarly explains that international applicants needing contributions below $40,000 face the greatest competition in its selection process.

These details are important for students who need almost complete funding. A university may technically meet full demonstrated need but still be highly selective about admitting students who require large financial aid packages.

Always look for the latest Common Data Set, international admissions statistics and financial aid reports before deciding where to apply.

Eligibility Criteria

General eligibility for international applicants

  • Applicants should be completing or have completed secondary school and be applying for their first undergraduate degree.
  • Applicants must satisfy the academic requirements of their chosen university.
  • Applicants must demonstrate sufficient English proficiency where required.
  • Students requesting need-based aid must submit the required financial information during the admission process.
  • Applicants should be prepared to submit academic transcripts, national examination results, recommendation letters and other required documents.
  • Applicants must complete the university-specific questions and supplemental essays.
  • International applicants must follow the institution's citizenship and residency classification rules.

Eligibility for refugee and displaced applicants

Refugee students living outside the United States may apply as international applicants according to their citizenship and the university's classification policy.

A refugee living in Kenya who holds South Sudanese citizenship, for example, should not automatically select the US refugee or asylee category in Common App. That category is intended for people who have the relevant US immigration status.

Common App generally classifies foreign nationals without US citizenship or permanent residency under the non-US citizenship option. Individual universities may request additional information about refugee status.

Refugee documentation may help universities understand an applicant's educational circumstances, displacement, interrupted schooling and financial situation. Submit identity and refugee documentation when requested through the university's official process.

Financial eligibility

Students applying for need-based aid should provide an accurate account of their family's financial situation. Universities may request:

  • Parent or guardian income information.
  • Employment or unemployment documentation.
  • Family financial statements.
  • Information about family members and dependants.
  • Bank statements or asset information where required.
  • Explanations of unusual financial circumstances.
  • Documents explaining displacement or refugee circumstances where relevant.

If your parents or guardians have no formal employment, do not invent income figures. Contact the financial aid office and ask which alternative documents or explanations they accept.

What Can Need-Based Financial Aid Cover?

Financial aid packages differ from one university to another. Depending on the institution's policy and your calculated need, assistance may include:

  • Tuition: The cost of academic instruction.
  • Accommodation: University housing or an allowance for approved accommodation.
  • Meals: University dining plans or an allowance for food.
  • Mandatory fees: Required university charges.
  • Books and supplies: Academic materials and other approved expenses.
  • Health insurance: Where included in the financial aid budget.
  • Personal expenses: An allowance for necessary living expenses.
  • Travel: Some universities provide travel allowances or other assistance.

Costs students should verify separately

  • International flight tickets.
  • Passport processing costs.
  • Student visa application expenses.
  • SEVIS fees and other immigration-related costs.
  • Travel to the US embassy or consulate.
  • Initial arrival expenses.
  • Accommodation during university holidays.
Do not assume every cost is covered.

Even a university that meets 100% of demonstrated financial need may calculate expenses differently from another university. Ask the financial aid office for a written explanation of the complete package before accepting admission.

Important Financial Aid Conditions

Apply for financial aid when submitting your admission application

Many US universities require international students to request financial assistance at the time they apply for admission.

Some institutions will not allow an international student who initially applied without requesting aid to apply for institutional need-based funding in subsequent years.

If you know that you cannot afford university without assistance, do not select the option indicating that you will not need aid simply to improve your admission chances.

Understand your expected family contribution

Full demonstrated need does not automatically mean that the university will expect your family to pay nothing.

Each institution uses its own financial aid formula to calculate what it believes a family can contribute. The result may differ significantly between universities.

If your family has no realistic ability to contribute, explain this accurately and provide supporting financial information. Ask whether the university can consider exceptional circumstances.

Do not rely on future scholarships to close a funding gap

If a university offers you partial funding, do not assume you will find another scholarship after arriving in the United States.

International students may have limited access to external funding, and some scholarships are restricted to students who are already enrolled.

Your safest approach is to obtain a complete and affordable financial aid package before committing to attend.

Step-by-Step Common App Application Process

  1. Create a Common App account. Visit the official website and register as a first-year applicant if you have not previously enrolled in a postsecondary institution.
  2. Complete your personal profile. Provide accurate personal, citizenship, education and family information.

Early Decision, Early Action and Regular Decision

Early Decision (ED)

Early Decision is generally binding. If admitted, you are expected to attend the institution and withdraw other applications, subject to the institution's agreement and financial aid release rules.

Students who need to compare several financial aid packages should be particularly careful before choosing Early Decision.

Early Action (EA)

Early Action is generally non-binding. Students can receive an admission decision earlier without committing to attend. However, some institutions have restrictive early-action rules.

Regular Decision (RD)

Regular Decision allows students to apply to multiple institutions and compare their admission and financial aid offers before making a final decision.

For applicants who require almost complete funding, Regular Decision can be a practical choice because it allows them to evaluate different offers.

Always read the individual university's application agreement before selecting an admission round.

How Universities Evaluate Applicants

Most selective US universities use holistic admission. This means they evaluate several aspects of an applicant's background rather than relying exclusively on examination grades.

  • Academic performance: Secondary school grades and academic preparation.
  • Academic potential: Ability to succeed in the intended undergraduate programme.
  • Essays: Personal experiences, goals, values and intellectual interests.
  • Recommendations: Teachers' and counsellors' assessments.
  • Extracurricular involvement: Leadership, community participation, projects and other activities.
  • Personal circumstances: Educational opportunities, family circumstances, displacement and other relevant context.
  • Financial need: Considered during admission at need-aware institutions.

Refugee applicants can explain interrupted education, limited access to learning resources and other circumstances that affected their academic journey.

However, personal circumstances do not automatically guarantee admission or a scholarship.

Application Fees and Fraud Prevention

Common App is free to create an account and complete the main application. Individual universities may charge application fees.

Students who cannot afford application fees should check whether Common App or the university provides a fee waiver. International applicants should confirm the specific policy before submitting.

  • Never pay an individual who promises guaranteed admission.
  • Do not pay unofficial agents to secure a scholarship.
  • Use official university websites and verified application portals.
  • Do not share passwords or sensitive documents with unverified contacts.
  • Be cautious of anyone promising a fully funded scholarship in exchange for money.

Universities make their own admission and financial aid decisions. No private individual can guarantee an admission offer.

About the Scholarship

The Albert Einstein German Academic Refugee Initiative (DAFI) is a tertiary education scholarship programme supported by UNHCR and its partners. Since 1992, it has helped refugees access higher education and develop the skills needed to contribute to their communities.

In Kenya, DAFI supports eligible refugee students pursuing higher education at participating institutions. The programme is particularly important for students who have completed secondary school but face financial barriers to university or other eligible tertiary studies.

DAFI is competitive, and the availability of scholarships depends on funding, the annual selection process, and the requirements of the country programme.

Eligibility Criteria

Applicants must meet the requirements in the current DAFI application announcement. These may include:

  • Being a refugee who meets the programme's status and documentation requirements.
  • Having completed secondary education and meeting the admission requirements of the intended tertiary institution.
  • Demonstrating strong academic potential and the ability to complete the chosen course.
  • Meeting the programme's financial-need and other selection criteria.
  • Choosing an eligible course and institution under the current call.
  • Submitting a complete application with the required supporting documents before the deadline.

Meeting the general criteria does not guarantee selection. Applicants should check the current Kenya-specific call for exact age, academic, residence, documentation, course, and institution requirements.

What the Scholarship Covers

DAFI support is intended to help scholars pursue their studies without being prevented by basic education and living expenses. Depending on the country programme and award, support may include:

  • University tuition and required academic fees.
  • Accommodation and food allowances.
  • Books, study materials, and other education-related expenses.
  • Local transportation.
  • Additional academic support, mentoring, or personal development opportunities.

The exact package varies by country and scholarship award. Applicants should confirm the benefits offered in the current Kenya call rather than assume every expense is covered in every case.

Documents to Prepare

The official application notice determines which documents are compulsory. Applicants may be asked to provide:

  • Recognised refugee identification or proof of refugee status.
  • Secondary school certificate and academic transcripts.
  • University admission letter, where required.
  • National or other identification documents, where applicable.
  • Academic references or recommendation letters, if requested.
  • A personal statement or motivation letter, if requested.
  • Any additional forms specified in the application announcement.

Do not submit this list as a substitute for the official checklist. Prepare documents according to the instructions for the current intake.

Application Period and Deadline

In Kenya, DAFI applications have commonly been announced around April or May. However, the opening date and deadline can change from year to year.

I have not confirmed an open Kenya DAFI application call for the current intake. Visit the official UNHCR Kenya education and announcements pages to check whether a new call has been published. Do not rely on deadlines from previous years.

How to Apply

  1. Read the DAFI information published by UNHCR Kenya.
  2. Check the latest scholarship announcement for eligibility, courses, institutions, and deadlines.

About the Programme

The Student Refugee Program (SRP) is managed by World University Service of Canada (WUSC), a Canadian international development organisation. Established in 1978, the programme combines access to post-secondary education with refugee resettlement to Canada.

It provides eligible young refugees with an opportunity to continue their education at participating Canadian universities, colleges, and CEGEPs. CEGEPs are post-secondary institutions found primarily in Quebec.

Unlike an ordinary scholarship, SRP also includes a resettlement pathway. Selected students are supported through Canada's Private Sponsorship of Refugees framework, subject to assessment and final approval by Canadian immigration authorities.

WUSC works with local partner organisations in countries where applications are accepted. In Canada, campus-based WUSC Local Committees help provide financial, academic, and social support to arriving students.

Important: SRP facilitates undergraduate study only. It is not a direct-entry master's or PhD scholarship, and selection by WUSC does not automatically guarantee approval to travel to Canada.

  • Lebanon

Applicants must be living in one of these participating countries and satisfy its residence, registration, academic, and other programme requirements. The programme is not open to applicants living anywhere in the world.

  • Be single, unmarried, and without children.
  • Face barriers to accessing or completing undergraduate post-secondary education.
  • Be considered eligible and suitable for resettlement under Canada's Private Sponsorship of Refugees framework.
  • Demonstrate maturity, self-reliance, and suitability for living and studying independently in Canada.

What the Programme Provides

SRP is not a conventional cash scholarship paid directly to a student. Support is organised through WUSC, participating educational institutions, and campus Local Committees.

During the initial 12-month sponsorship period, support can include:

  • Free or reduced tuition and accommodation costs, arranged through the programme and participating institution.
  • Financial assistance for basic necessities.
  • Academic support and guidance as students adjust to their studies.
  • Social support from a campus-based WUSC Local Committee.
  • Assistance with integration into the Canadian university or college community.
  • Support with the programme's resettlement and pre-departure process.

The financial arrangements may include in-kind assistance rather than unrestricted cash payments. Applicants should not assume that every personal expense will be paid directly or that the same package applies at every institution.

After the initial 12 months, students are expected to work towards greater financial independence. Possible sources of continued support include institutional bursaries, scholarships, student loans where appropriate, and permitted employment income. Students should discuss their options with their institution and Local Committee.

Selection and Shortlisting

After the application deadline, WUSC reviews applications to determine whether candidates satisfy the programme's eligibility requirements. Only shortlisted applicants are contacted to progress to subsequent selection stages.

The process is competitive and considers eligibility, academic readiness, the barriers applicants face in accessing higher education, and suitability for the programme and resettlement. The precise stages and assessments are determined by WUSC's selection process for the relevant intake.

Submitting an application does not guarantee an interview, shortlisting, selection, admission to a particular institution, or approval for resettlement.

WUSC states that it does not set nationality-based quotas for applicants. Refugees of different nationalities may apply as long as they meet the requirements of a participating country.

Resettlement to Canada

A distinctive feature of SRP is its connection to Canada's Private Sponsorship of Refugees framework. WUSC selects eligible candidates and supports the resettlement process with its partners in Canada.

The process can take two years or longer. Final immigration decisions are made by Canadian immigration authorities, not by WUSC alone. Candidates must satisfy the relevant immigration requirements and admissibility checks before travel is approved.

Students who are approved through the programme arrive in Canada as permanent residents. Permanent residence gives them a different immigration status from that of an ordinary international student. They may later be eligible to apply for Canadian citizenship if they satisfy the applicable legal requirements.

Selection by WUSC is not a guarantee of a visa, travel approval, or admission to Canada. If Canadian authorities refuse a candidate's application or deny entry, the candidate's participation may be cancelled.

About UNICORE

UNICORE stands for University Corridors for Refugees. It is an initiative coordinated by the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), in partnership with Italian universities, the Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation, and other organisations.

The initiative aims to expand access to higher education for refugees living in countries of first asylum. It creates a safe and regular pathway for selected students to travel to Italy and continue their university education.

Participating universities offer master's scholarships and manage their own academic selection processes. UNHCR and programme partners support the wider initiative and refugee verification arrangements.

Unlike an ordinary international student admission route, UNICORE is specifically designed for eligible refugees who meet the requirements of the relevant call and participating university.

The programme does not automatically grant a place to every refugee applicant. Selection is competitive, and applicants are assessed according to academic merit, motivation, programme suitability, and the requirements published by the university.

Official UNICORE Programme Information

Level of Study and Duration

UNICORE primarily supports eligible refugees pursuing a second-cycle university qualification, which corresponds to a master's degree in the Italian higher education system.

  • Applicants must hold officially recognized refugee status (in collaboration with UNHCR) and reside in one of the designated host countries (e.g., Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia, South Africa, Nigeria, Mozambique, Tanzania, Zambia, Zimbabwe).
  • Candidates must hold a Bachelor's degree awarded by a recognized higher education institution (typically obtained within the last 5 years).
  • Applicants must demonstrate strong academic achievement with a Minimum Cumulative Grade Point Average (GPA) of at least 3.0 on a 4.0 scale (or equivalent to at least 24/30 under the Italian grading system).
  • Candidates must meet the specific entry and academic course requirements for their chosen Master's degree program at participating Italian host universities.
  • Applicants must have never before been enrolled in a degree program at any of the partner universities participating in the UNICORE project.
  • Candidates must demonstrate English language proficiency at CEFR Level B2 or higher through official test certificates or proof of an English-medium undergraduate degree.
  • Applicants may apply to a maximum of two participating Italian universities per call for applications cycle.
  • Candidates must successfully pass both the initial documentation evaluation and an online interview conducted by the university's expert selection committee.

Applicants must hold a qualification that is valid for admission to their chosen master's programme. A bachelor's degree alone does not guarantee eligibility: the degree, grades, subject background, and any additional admission requirements must satisfy the university's rules.

UNICORE is not a general undergraduate scholarship for students who have only completed secondary school.
