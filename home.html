Common Mistakes

  • Missing deadlines
  • Submitting wrong documents
  • Writing weak essays
  • Ignoring eligibility requirements
  • Not proofreading applications
  • Ignoring age restrictions.
  • Missing country-specific requirements.
  • Waiting until the deadline.
  • Uploading blurry scans.
  • Using expired passports.
  • Uploading the wrong file.
  • Giving files incorrect names.
  • Missing signatures.
  • Forgetting certified copies when required.
  • Uploading files larger than the allowed size.
  • Copying essays from the internet.
  • Reusing the same essay without tailoring it.
  • Not answering the prompt/not following instructions when writing essays.
  • Exaggerating achievements.
  • Poor grammar and spelling.
  • Asking referees at the last minute.
  • Using personal friends or relatives instead of appropriate referees.
  • Submitting incomplete recommendation forms.
  • Using an unprofessional email address(for example:babyface@gmail.com). It is okay if you have to create a new for the sole purpose of the application.
  • Typing incorrect personal details.
  • Forgetting to save progress.
  • Not reviewing the application before submitting.
  • Ignoring confirmation emails.
  • Not researching the scholarship.
  • Arriving late for interviews.
  • Giving inconsistent answers during interviews.
  • Memorising responses instead of understanding your story.
  • Dressing inappropriately for professional interviews.
  • Not preparing questions for the interviewers.
  • Missing acceptance deadlines.
  • Delaying visa applications.
  • Ignoring pre-departure instructions.
  • Failing to submit requested documents after being selected.

Tips for a Successful Application.

Start Your Application Early

Begin your application weeks before the deadline. This gives you enough time to prepare documents, request recommendation letters, write strong essays, and correct any mistakes.

Tailor Every Application

Avoid sending the same personal statement to every scholarship. Show how your goals, experiences, and values align with the specific scholarship's mission.

Read Every Requirement Carefully

Check the eligibility criteria, required documents, word limits, and submission instructions. Missing even one requirement could result in automatic disqualification.

Proofread Before Submitting

Review your application for spelling, grammar, incorrect dates, and missing information. If possible, ask a teacher, mentor, or friend to review it before submission.

Use a Professional Email Address

Create an email address using your real name. Avoid nicknames or usernames that may appear unprofessional in official scholarship applications.

Keep Digital Copies of Documents

Save all your essays, certificates, transcripts, recommendation letters, passport scans, and confirmation emails in one organised folder for future applications.

Track Your Applications

Keep a record of scholarship names, deadlines, login details, documents submitted, and application status to avoid missing important updates.

Choose Referees Early

Contact your teachers, lecturers, employers, or supervisors well in advance. Give them enough time to prepare thoughtful and personalised recommendation letters.

Be Honest in Your Application

Never exaggerate achievements or submit false information. Scholarship providers often verify academic records, work experience, and recommendation letters.

Prepare for Scholarship Interviews

Practise answering common interview questions, research the scholarship organisation, and be ready to explain your goals, achievements, and future plans confidently.
