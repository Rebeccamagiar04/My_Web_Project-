Understanding the Application Process

Every scholarship is different. Requirements, deadlines, application procedures and selection processes can vary between programs.

Always read the official scholarship information carefully and use this guide as a general reference.

1. Research the Scholarship

Before starting an application, take time to understand the scholarship and what it offers. Do not apply simply because you found a scholarship with an attractive funding package.

Check the official website and carefully look for the following information:

  • Who is eligible to apply?
  • Which countries or regions are eligible?
  • What level of study is supported?
  • Are there age requirements?
  • Are there restrictions on the field of study?
  • What expenses does the scholarship cover?
  • What documents are required?
  • What is the application deadline?
  • How should the application be submitted?

2. Check Your Eligibility

Carefully compare your situation with the scholarship's eligibility requirements before spending time on the application.

Eligibility may depend on factors such as nationality, residence, age, academic background, study level, previous education or other conditions.

Go through each requirement one by one. If the scholarship provides an eligibility checklist, use it to confirm that you meet the requirements.

3. Understand the Admission Requirements

Some scholarships require applicants to apply for university admission separately, while others have their own admission process.

Read both the scholarship and university instructions carefully so you understand whether you need to apply for admission, the scholarship, or both.

4. Prepare Your Documents

Start gathering your documents early. Some documents may take time to obtain, replace, translate or certify.

Common documents may include:

  • Identification documents
  • Academic certificates
  • Academic transcripts
  • Language proficiency results
  • Curriculum Vitae (CV)
  • Personal statement or motivation letter
  • Recommendation letters
  • Proof of financial circumstances, when required
  • Portfolio or other supporting documents, when required

Requirements differ between scholarships, so do not prepare documents based only on another applicant's experience.

5. Create Your Application Account

If the scholarship uses an online application system, you may need to create an account before beginning your application.

  • Use an email address that you check regularly.
  • Keep your login details secure.
  • Use your real and correct personal information.
  • Read the instructions before starting the form.
  • Save your progress regularly.

6. Complete the Application Form

Complete each section carefully and provide accurate information.

Make sure that your personal information, education history, dates and other details are consistent throughout the application and supporting documents.

  • Read each question carefully.
  • Answer every required question.
  • Do not guess information you do not know.
  • Check spelling of names and institutions.
  • Keep important dates consistent.
  • Save your progress if the system allows it.

7. Write Your Essays and Personal Statements

Essays and personal statements give the scholarship committee an opportunity to understand your experiences, goals and reasons for applying.

Before writing, make sure you understand exactly what the question is asking.

  • Read the prompt carefully.
  • Plan your main points before writing.
  • Use your own experiences and examples.
  • Explain your goals clearly.
  • Connect your experiences to your future goals.
  • Stay within the required word limit.
  • Proofread your work carefully.

Avoid copying essays from the internet or using someone else's story as your own.

8. Request Recommendation Letters

If recommendation letters are required, contact your referees early enough to give them time to prepare and submit their recommendations.

Choose people who are appropriate for the scholarship and who can genuinely speak about your academic work, character, experience or potential.

  • Ask before the deadline approaches.
  • Give your referee the relevant scholarship information.
  • Provide your CV or other useful information if appropriate.
  • Confirm how the recommendation should be submitted.
  • Check that the recommendation has been submitted when possible.

9. Upload Your Documents Carefully

Before uploading a document, check that it is the correct file and meets the application's technical requirements.

  • Use the required file format.
  • Check the maximum file size.
  • Make sure scans are clear and readable.
  • Upload the correct document.
  • Check whether signatures are required.
  • Check whether certified copies are required.
  • Make sure documents have not expired when an expiry date matters.
  • Use clear and appropriate file names.

10. Review Everything Before Submitting

Do not rush to click the submit button. Take time to review the entire application.

Check that:

  • Your personal details are correct.
  • Your education information is accurate.
  • All required questions have been answered.
  • Your essays answer the required questions.
  • Your recommendation letters are complete.
  • All required documents have been uploaded.
  • Files meet the required format and size.
  • There are no spelling or grammar errors.
  • You have followed all application instructions.

11. Submit Before the Deadline

Do not wait until the final hours to submit your application.

Online systems can experience technical problems, internet interruptions or heavy traffic near deadlines.

Whenever possible, submit your application early enough to deal with unexpected problems.

12. Keep Your Confirmation and Track Your Application

After submitting your application, keep evidence that it was successfully submitted.

  • Save confirmation emails.
  • Save your application reference number if provided.
  • Keep a copy of your submitted application.
  • Check your email regularly.
  • Check your spam or junk folder.
  • Monitor the application portal when applicable.

13. Prepare for an Interview

Some scholarships include an interview as part of the selection process.

Review the application you submitted and make sure you understand the experiences, achievements and goals you discussed.

  • Review your application.
  • Understand your own story.
  • Practice answering common questions.
  • Give honest and consistent answers.
  • Avoid memorising answers word for word.
  • Prepare a few questions for the interviewers.
  • Be punctual.
  • Dress appropriately for the interview setting.

14. If You Are Selected

Being selected is not always the final step. Scholarship providers may require additional documents or actions before your award is confirmed.

  • Read the selection or award letter carefully.
  • Accept the scholarship before the stated deadline.
  • Submit any additional documents requested.
  • Complete university admission requirements when applicable.
  • Follow visa and travel instructions when applicable.
  • Follow pre-departure instructions.
  • Keep copies of important documents and communications.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Small mistakes can affect an application. Review these common problems before submitting your scholarship application.

Eligibility & Research

  • Ignoring eligibility requirements.
  • Ignoring age restrictions.
  • Missing country-specific requirements.
  • Not researching the scholarship.
  • Missing deadlines.

Documents & Uploads

  • Submitting the wrong documents.
  • Uploading blurry scans.
  • Using expired documents when a valid document is required.
  • Uploading the wrong file.
  • Giving files incorrect names.
  • Missing signatures.
  • Forgetting certified copies when required.
  • Uploading files larger than the allowed size.

Essays & Personal Statements

  • Writing weak essays.
  • Copying essays from the internet.
  • Reusing the same essay without tailoring it.
  • Not answering the prompt.
  • Not following essay instructions.
  • Exaggerating achievements.
  • Poor grammar and spelling.

Recommendation Letters

  • Asking referees at the last minute.
  • Using personal friends or relatives instead of appropriate referees.
  • Submitting incomplete recommendation forms.

Application Form

  • Using an unprofessional email address.
  • Typing incorrect personal details.
  • Forgetting to save progress.
  • Not reviewing the application before submitting.
  • Ignoring confirmation emails.

Interviews

  • Arriving late for interviews.
  • Giving inconsistent answers during interviews.
  • Memorising responses instead of understanding your story.
  • Dressing inappropriately for professional interviews.
  • Not preparing questions for the interviewers.

After Selection

  • Missing acceptance deadlines.
  • Delaying visa applications.
  • Ignoring pre-departure instructions.
  • Failing to submit requested documents after being selected.
