Reminder

  • Keep your CV clear, professional, well-organized, and easy to read.
  • Include your education, academic achievements, relevant skills, projects, activities, leadership roles, volunteering, and experience where applicable.
  • List your educational qualifications in reverse chronological order, starting with the most recent.
  • Highlight academic strengths, awards, competition results, research, or independent projects that demonstrate your potential.
  • Use specific examples and achievements instead of vague statements. Explain what you accomplished and, where possible, the results.
  • Include relevant extracurricular activities and community involvement, especially where they demonstrate leadership, initiative, teamwork, or commitment.
  • Tailor your CV to the scholarship, university, or academic opportunity by emphasizing the qualifications and experiences most relevant to its selection criteria.
  • Include dates and brief descriptions of your responsibilities or contributions where they help explain your experience.
  • Be honest about your qualifications, achievements, and skills. Never include information you cannot explain or verify.
  • Keep formatting, headings, dates, and spacing consistent throughout the document.
  • Check your contact information, spelling, grammar, and document layout before submitting.
  • Follow the provider's instructions regarding CV length, file format, and required information.

Reminder

  • Explain clearly why you are applying for the scholarship, programme, or academic opportunity.
  • Show that you understand what the opportunity offers and why it is suitable for your goals.
  • Connect your educational background, experiences, interests, and achievements to the opportunity.
  • Explain why you chose your field of study and how it relates to your long-term academic or career plans.
  • Use specific examples to demonstrate your commitment, initiative, resilience, curiosity, or interest in your chosen field.
  • Explain what you hope to learn, contribute, and achieve if selected.
  • Show why the particular institution or programme is a good fit for your goals instead of relying on generic statements.
  • If relevant, explain how the scholarship would help you access educational opportunities and work toward your goals.
  • Organize your letter into clear paragraphs with a logical introduction, main discussion, and conclusion.
  • Use a respectful, professional tone while allowing your genuine personality and motivation to come through.
  • Avoid repeating your CV word for word. Use the letter to explain the meaning behind your experiences and achievements.
  • Follow the required word limit and any instructions provided by the scholarship provider.
  • Proofread carefully to correct spelling, grammar, repetition, and unclear sentences before submitting.

Reminder

  • Ask your recommender well in advance so they have enough time to prepare a thoughtful letter before the deadline.
  • Choose someone who knows your academic work, abilities, character, or professional contributions well and who meets the programme's referee requirements.
  • Tell your recommender the name of the scholarship, programme, and institution, and explain why you are applying.
  • Share the selection criteria and any instructions about the letter's length, format, content, or submission procedure.
  • Encourage your recommender to assess your potential using accurate descriptions of your achievements in the classroom, your community, and any relevant workplace or other setting.
  • Where appropriate and supported by evidence, the referee can quantify your achievements by mentioning grades, project results, responsibilities, participation, or measurable contributions.
  • Encourage specific examples rather than general praise. For example, the referee could describe your performance on a particular project, your contribution as part of a team, or how you solved a difficult problem.
  • The letter should explain the relationship between the referee and the candidate, including how they know you, their position, how long they have known you, and the context in which they have observed your work.
  • Ask the referee to describe relevant qualities they have genuinely observed, such as intellectual curiosity, discipline, teamwork, leadership, initiative, creativity, or perseverance.
  • The recommendation should explain how your demonstrated abilities and potential could be developed through participation in the particular programme at the particular institution.
  • Where relevant, the referee can explain how you may contribute to the institution, programme, academic community, or wider society.
  • Provide your academic CV, relevant achievements, project details, or personal statement if these would help the referee understand your background and goals.
  • Confirm whether the referee must upload the letter directly, submit it through an application portal, email it to the institution, or provide it in another specified format.
  • Respect the referee's independence. They must provide their own honest assessment and approve the final letter. Do not invent claims or submit a letter without their permission.
  • Follow up politely if necessary, especially as the deadline approaches, and thank your recommender for their time and support.

Reminder

  • Download the study plan and print it before using it so you can complete the sections and track your progress effectively.
  • Fill in the relevant sections as you study rather than waiting until the end.
  • Set clear, realistic learning goals and break large topics into smaller, manageable tasks.
  • Allocate study time according to the difficulty of each topic, your available hours, and upcoming deadlines.
  • Include time for revision, assignments, practice questions, and reviewing difficult concepts.
  • Mark tasks as completed only after you have finished them, and identify topics that need more attention.
  • Review your progress regularly and adjust your schedule when your priorities or circumstances change.
  • Keep the completed plan somewhere safe and accessible so you can refer to it and monitor your progress.
  • Use the plan as a guide rather than a rigid schedule. If you fall behind, reorganize your tasks and continue.

Reminder

  • Tell your own story rather than simply listing your qualifications and achievements.
  • Explain how your experiences, interests, challenges, opportunities, or responsibilities have shaped your goals and aspirations.
  • Choose experiences that are relevant to the scholarship, programme, or question being asked.
  • Use concrete examples to demonstrate your abilities, decisions, growth, achievements, or commitment.
  • Explain what you learned from important experiences and how that learning influenced your decisions or future plans.
  • Connect your background and goals to the opportunity, showing why the programme is relevant to the future you want to build.
  • Describe your academic interests and future ambitions clearly, and explain how you intend to work toward them.
  • Where appropriate, discuss the contributions you hope to make to your field, institution, or community.
  • Be honest and authentic. Do not invent experiences, exaggerate achievements, or include personal details that do not help answer the prompt.
  • Avoid clichés and generic statements. Use meaningful examples and your own voice to make your statement specific to you.
  • Make sure you answer every part of the question and follow any instructions about structure, content, and word count.
  • Organize your ideas logically, use clear paragraphs, and make sure each paragraph contributes to your main message.
  • Proofread for spelling, grammar, clarity, repetition, and consistency before submitting.

Reminder

  • Download the scholarship application checklist and print it so you can mark each task as you complete it.
  • Write the scholarship name and application deadline in the spaces provided before you begin.
  • Read through the entire checklist first to understand the stages of the application process.
  • Use the checklist to track eligibility requirements, required documents, application preparation, submission, and follow-up.
  • Tick a box only after completing the corresponding task, and review unfinished items regularly.
  • Check the scholarship provider's official instructions because requirements differ between scholarships and institutions.
  • Start early enough to request recommendation letters, obtain academic records, arrange translations or certification if required, and resolve possible technical problems.
  • Keep the printed checklist somewhere safe and accessible alongside your application documents.
  • Review the checklist before submission to reduce the risk of missing information or required documents.
  • After submitting, record any confirmation details and note important dates for interviews, results, or additional requirements.
  • Remember that the checklist is a planning tool and does not replace the scholarship provider's official instructions.

Reminder

  • Focus on skills, experience, and achievements relevant to the job, internship, or professional opportunity.
  • Place your strongest and most relevant qualifications near the beginning so employers can identify your suitability quickly.
  • Include your contact information, education, work experience, technical and transferable skills, projects, volunteering, and certifications where relevant.
  • Describe your responsibilities clearly, and highlight accomplishments or results wherever possible.
  • Use specific examples to show how you applied your skills, solved problems, supported a team, or completed a project.
  • Use clear, professional language and action-oriented descriptions instead of vague statements or unnecessary details.
  • Tailor your CV to the specific position by highlighting the experience and skills that match the job description.
  • Keep the formatting consistent and easy to scan, with clear headings, readable spacing, and consistent dates.
  • Be truthful about your qualifications, employment history, skills, and achievements.
  • Check your contact details, spelling, grammar, dates, and the order of your information before sending.
  • Follow the employer's instructions regarding CV length, file type, and submission method.
  • Save the final version with a professional filename that identifies the document clearly.

Explore by category

Every scholarship and university has different requirements. Use this guide to understand the types of documents you may be asked to provide. Select a category to see common documents, what they are used for, and practical tips.

01

Identification & Status

Documents you may need

  • Passport
  • Refugee identification document
  • UNHCR documentation
  • National identification card
  • Birth certificate
  • Proof of nationality
  • Proof of legal residence
  • Other official identification documents, where applicable

What is it?

Documents that may be used to confirm your identity, nationality, or legal status.

What may you need them for?

  • University applications
  • Scholarship applications
  • Visa applications

💡 Tips

  • Don't have one of these documents? Check whether the institution accepts alternative documentation.
  • Check your name carefully and make sure it is consistent across your documents.

02

Academic Documents

Documents you may need

  • Secondary school certificate
  • Academic results slip
  • Academic transcript
  • School leaving certificate
  • Diploma or degree certificate
  • Grading scale
  • Other academic records, where applicable

What is it?

Documents that show your educational background, academic results, qualifications, and previous study.

What may you need them for?

  • University admission
  • Scholarship applications
  • Academic verification

💡 Tips

  • Keep clear digital copies of your academic documents.
  • Check whether documents need to be translated or certified.

03

Language Proficiency

Documents you may need

  • IELTS results
  • TOEFL results
  • Duolingo English Test results
  • Other accepted language-test results
  • Proof of English-medium education, where accepted

What is it?

Documents that show your ability to study or communicate in the language required by a university or scholarship.

What may you need them for?

  • University admission
  • Scholarship applications
  • Meeting language requirements

💡 Tips

  • Check which language tests your institution accepts.
  • Check the required score and validity period before testing.

04

Essays & Statements

Documents you may need

  • Motivation letter
  • Personal statement
  • Statement of purpose
  • Study plan
  • Scholarship essays
  • Other written responses requested by the provider

What is it?

Written documents that help an institution understand your goals, experiences, motivation, and plans for the future.

What may you need them for?

  • Scholarship applications
  • University applications
  • Explaining your academic or career goals

💡 Tips

  • Answer the exact question being asked.
  • Use specific experiences rather than general statements.

05

Recommendations

Documents you may need

  • Recommendation letters
  • Academic references
  • Teacher or counsellor references
  • Employer references, where applicable
  • Referee contact information

What is it?

Letters or references from people who can speak about your academic background, character, skills, or experience.

What may you need them for?

  • Scholarship applications
  • University admission
  • Supporting your experience and achievements

💡 Tips

  • Ask your referee early rather than waiting until the deadline.
  • Choose someone who knows your work or experience well.

06

Experience & Portfolio

Documents you may need

  • CV or résumé
  • Work experience records
  • Volunteer certificates
  • Leadership certificates
  • Portfolio
  • Awards and achievements
  • Proof of extracurricular activities
  • Research work or publications, where applicable

What is it?

Documents and materials that show what you have done outside your formal academic records.

What may you need them for?

  • Scholarship applications
  • University applications
  • Showing skills, leadership, or experience

💡 Tips

  • Keep certificates and evidence of important activities in one place.
  • Focus on experiences that are relevant to the application.

07

Health & Travel

Documents you may need

  • Medical examination form or certificate
  • Vaccination records
  • Health insurance documents
  • Visa documents
  • Admission or offer letter
  • Proof of funds, where required
  • Accommodation confirmation
  • Travel insurance, where required
  • Visa photographs

What is it?

Documents that may be required when preparing for travel, visa processing, health checks, or relocation for study.

What may you need them for?

  • Visa applications
  • Travel preparation
  • University or scholarship requirements

💡 Tips

  • Check the current requirements for your destination.
  • Start preparing travel documents early if you are admitted.
