Recommendation Letter Email

Subject: Recommendation Letter Request for [Name of Scholarship/Program] – [Your Full Name] Dear [Title and Last Name], I hope this email finds you well. I am writing to ask if you would feel comfortable writing a strong letter of recommendation for my application to the [Name of Scholarship/Program]. Having thoroughly enjoyed your [Course Name / Project] and gained [mention specific skill], I believe your perspective would add immense value to my application. About the Scholarship: • Target Field: [Briefly state what it supports] • Submission Deadline: [Insert Date] To make this process as easy as possible for you, I have attached my updated Academic CV and a draft of my personal statement. Summary of My Key Contributions: • Grade/Performance: Received an [Grade] in [Course] during [Semester/Year]. • Key Project: Completed [Briefly name major project]. If you are able to support my application, please let me know by [Insert Date], and I will forward the official submission link. Warm regards, [Your Full Name] [Your Phone Number] [Your Degree Program]

Personal Statement Outline

Section 1: The Hook & Core Purpose (Paragraph 1) •Opening Hook: Start with a compelling narrative, pivotal moment, or central passion driving your goals. (Avoid cliché quotes or opening with "My name is..."). •Thesis Statement: Explicitly state what field you want to study and your long-term objective. •Key Prompt to Answer: What specific vision or problem drives your academic pursuit? Section 2: Academic Background & Skills (Paragraphs 2–3) •Academic Milestones: Detail relevant coursework, research projects, or self-directed learning relevant to your chosen field. •Overcoming Challenges: Briefly explain how you navigated academic or financial constraints, highlighting resilience and resourcefulness. •Key Prompt to Answer: What concrete evidence proves you have the intellectual capacity to succeed in this program? Section 3: Leadership & Community Impact (Paragraph 4) •Real-World Initiative: Describe a leadership experience, volunteer effort, or community initiative you spearheaded or significantly contributed to. •Measurable Results: Quantify your impact (e.g., "Trained 15 students," "Organized workshops for over 50 community members"). •Key Prompt to Answer: How do you use your skills to serve others and create tangible change? Section 4: Future Vision & Program Fit (Paragraph 5) •Short- & Long-Term Goals: Explain what you plan to do immediately after completing your studies, and where you see yourself in 5–10 years. •Why This Specific Opportunity: Name specific professors, courses, or values of the scholarship provider that align with your mission. •Key Prompt to Answer: Why is this scholarship the exact bridge you need to reach your goals, and how will society benefit from investing in you?
